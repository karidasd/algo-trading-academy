Trader's & Quant Dictionary

Over 40+ fundamental terms, algorithmic metrics, market microstructure definitions, and prop firm terminology.

Pip (Point in Percentage)

The smallest standardized unit of price change in Forex (usually 0.0001 for EUR/USD). For a standard 1.0 lot, 1 pip equals roughly $10 USD.

Slippage

The difference between the expected price of a trade and the price at which the order is actually executed by the broker or DEX pool due to volatility or latency.

Spread

The gap between the Bid (highest price a buyer will pay) and the Ask (lowest price a seller will accept). It represents the immediate cost of entering a position.

Sharpe Ratio

A metric measuring risk-adjusted return: (Portfolio Return - Risk Free Rate) / Standard Deviation. A Sharpe ratio above 2.0 indicates an institutional-grade algorithmic strategy.

Max Drawdown (MDD)

The maximum observed percentage loss from a peak to a trough of a portfolio before a new peak is attained. Crucial for passing Prop Firm challenges (often capped at 10%).

MEV (Maximal Extractable Value)

In decentralized finance (Solana / Ethereum), value extracted by block producers or searchers through front-running, back-running, or sandwiching decentralized trades.

Order Block (OB)

Institutional footprint on a candlestick chart where banks or smart money accumulated large positions before an explosive expansion in price.

Leverage

A loan provided by a broker enabling traders to control larger position sizes with small capital (e.g. 1:100 leverage turns a $1,000 deposit into $100,000 purchasing power).

Mean Reversion

A financial theory suggesting that asset prices and historical returns eventually return back to their long-term average or mean over time.

Trailing Stop Loss

A dynamic risk management order that automatically shifts in the direction of a winning trade by a predetermined distance, locking in profits as price advances.

REST API & WebSockets

Protocols enabling automated bots to communicate with broker servers: REST is used for transactional execution (Buy/Sell), while WebSockets stream continuous tick-level live prices.

The 90/90/90 Rule

A widely cited financial market statistic: 90% of new retail traders lose 90% of their starting capital within their first 90 days due to over-leveraging and lack of emotional discipline.
