Quantitative Indicators: Moving Averages, RSI & MACD

Deconstruct mathematical indicator formulas, avoid lag traps, and write algorithms combining momentum oscillators with trend filters.

1. The Math Behind the Indicators

Simple Moving Average (SMA) vs Exponential (EMA)

An SMA assigns equal weight to all data points over period \(N\). An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) assigns greater weight to recent prices using a multiplier \(\alpha = \frac{2}{N+1}\), making it significantly more responsive to sudden market turns.

Relative Strength Index (RSI)

Measures momentum on an oscillator scale of 0 to 100:

\[RSI = 100 - \left(\frac{100}{1 + RS}\right) \quad \text{where } RS = \frac{\text{Average Gain}}{\text{Average Loss}}\]
  • RSI < 30: Oversold condition (potential mean-reversion buy).
  • RSI > 70: Overbought condition (potential mean-reversion sell).
