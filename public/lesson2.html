Technical Analysis: Support & Resistance

Welcome to the Technical Analysis chapter. Today we focus on one of the most powerful and universally applied concepts in financial trading: Support and Resistance (S&R). Whether you are scalping on a 1-minute chart or investing on a weekly chart, these invisible barriers govern price action.

1. What are Support and Resistance?

Imagine a bouncing ball in an enclosed room. It hits the floor and bounces up; it hits the ceiling and bounces down. Market prices behave similarly within trading ranges.

  • Support (The Floor): A price level where a downtrend tends to pause or reverse due to a concentration of demand (buying interest). When the price drops to this level, buyers step in, thinking it's "cheap."
  • Resistance (The Ceiling): A price level where an uptrend tends to pause or reverse due to a concentration of supply (selling interest). When the price rises to this level, sellers step in, thinking it's "expensive."

A key rule of thumb in technical analysis is: Once broken, Support becomes Resistance, and Resistance becomes Support.

"Don't anticipate and move without market confirmation - being a little late in your trade is your insurance that you are right or wrong." — Jesse Livermore

2. The Strategy: Range Trading Bots

In algorithmic trading, we can program a bot to identify these "floors and ceilings" dynamically by scanning the highest highs and lowest lows over a specific timeframe window.

Our strategy rules for the Botaki simulator:

  • Continuously track the rolling highest and lowest price to define the Resistance and Support.
  • If the price drops near the Support level, execute a BUY, expecting it to bounce up (reversal).
  • If the price rises near the Resistance level, execute a SELL, expecting it to drop down.
  • Implement strict Take Profit (TP) and Stop Loss (SL) logic to secure profits and prevent catastrophic losses if a "breakout" occurs.
