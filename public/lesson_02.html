Financial Markets: Instruments & Terminology

A financial market is a broad term describing any marketplace where buyers and sellers participate in the trade of assets such as equities, bonds, currencies, and derivatives.

Types of Financial Markets

  • Stock Market: Where investors buy and sell shares of publicly traded companies (e.g., Apple, Tesla).
  • Bond Market: Where investors buy debt securities issued by governments or corporations.
  • Commodities Market: The buying and selling of natural resources like Gold, Oil, and Agricultural products.
  • Cryptocurrency Market: The digital asset space where tokens like Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana are traded 24/7.

Key Trading Terminology

Before diving deep into algorithmic trading, you must speak the language of the market:

  • Liquidity: How easily an asset can be bought or sold without affecting its price. Forex and large-cap stocks are highly liquid.
  • Volatility: The rate at which the price of an asset increases or decreases. High volatility means large price swings (ideal for bots, but risky).
  • Spread: The difference between the Bid (sell) price and the Ask (buy) price.
  • Leverage: Using borrowed capital from a broker to increase the potential return of an investment (Warning: also magnifies losses).
"In trading, the impossible happens about twice a year." — Henri Fayol

Understanding these instruments allows you to decide what your trading bot will specialize in. A bot built for the highly volatile Crypto market requires entirely different risk management than a bot trading blue-chip stocks.
